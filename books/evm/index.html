About the Book

Employing Variant Minds is a practical guide for people managers and HR staff who want their workplace to work for autistic, ADHD and AuDHD people. It starts from a plain fact: those people already work for you. Most haven't told anyone, and many don't know themselves. You can't tell who they are, and you shouldn't try. So whatever you build has to work without knowing.

The book makes the business case, and shows what doing nothing already costs. It shows what ADHD, autism and AuDHD look like at a desk rather than in a clinic, and the strengths each brings, from deep focus to working well under pressure to calm in a crisis. Then it follows the work through the door, where hiring screens out the people it needs, to the desk, where adjustments and an open mind keep them, and into the HR system, from the annual review to the "team player".

This second edition is a new book. It is the print companion to the Variant Minds half-day training session, and it reads just as well on its own. It's plain, practical and built on published research, and it says so when the research is thin. It sets out the legal floor in plain terms for Australia, the UK, the US and Canada. It isn't a clinical text, and it isn't a guide to spotting who is neurodivergent.

Design for the person who will never tell you, and you build a workplace that works better for everyone already in it.

About the Author

Silvino Rodrigues brings a rare combination of lived experience and formal expertise to the neurodivergence conversation. With over thirty years of corporate technology and tertiary studies spanning electronics engineering, software development, financial management, an MBA, and postgraduate work in adult education and psychology, Silvino writes with both analytical rigour and hard-earned personal insight.

A late-life discovery of autism and ADHD sparked a new direction: understanding why so many neurodivergent adults - and those who live and work alongside them - struggle to bridge a gap neither side can fully see. These books provide partners, colleagues, and employers seeking genuine understanding with clear explanations of how variant minds work.

Silvino now dedicates time to neurodivergent research, writing, and advocacy and is available for corporate and public speaking engagements on neurodivergence in relationships, workplaces, and everyday life.
